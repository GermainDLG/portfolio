HCII Research under Canwen Wang

August 2026

In this research, I evaluated three agents against a given set of human-agent interaction design principles through conversations designed to probe their behavior. I then made adherence judgements in a structured evaluation spreadsheet, and supported each decision with written justification and screenshot proof. As an evaluator, I came up with a set of scenarios and prompted each agent until they had reached a critical point. From there, I would follow my created scenario and ask the agent certain questions to incite a response that would show whether it follows a principle or not, for example how the agent prioritizes different levels of failure. I would record its response and judge whether or not it followed a principle given the definition of a principle.

Quicksilver Labs Table Pairing

Summer 2026

Part of my work at Quicksilver Labs was designing an interface that allowed a grader to review suggestions from a database schema-matching algorithm, and the perform actions on them such as accepting, rejecting, or flagging. Below you will find the 6 iterations of my work, created over a span of 2 weeks. For this project, I performed research on existing user flows following similar structures, designed a specification document focused around the needs of the users, designed interfaces in Figma, and used AI to rapidly implement my interfaces into testable prototypes to get feedback. By following down the list, it's clear that the designs evolved over time because of the feedback I received; My team and I discussed what was or wasn't working, research we had found, and identified the needs of the users. For example, the change from Github table to Popup action was motivated largely by Fitt's Law. By moving the continue button towards the center of the screen, the user would spend less time going back and forth between buttons. This change also reduced the visual clutter across the dashboard. Ultimately, we decided on the heatmap idea. We had found previous research on a similar idea that showed how effective this method was, and had assessed that the current iteration was at a local maxima. The biggest tradeoff that remains is that the heatmap still has a lot of scrolling and can get unwieldy. While we recognized this, we also found that it was the most effective iteration, and so we stopped there due to time constraint.

Space / orbital forecasting

Summer 2026

I did this research for Quicksilver Labs over the summer to evaluate whether Time-Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) could be used in another project I was working on. View the link below to see some of the graphs and research I did! The biggest concern among the TSFMs is that they could not accept much data. Moirai could not accept more than 512 data points and predict more than 64 at a time. Chronos-2 was slightly better, taking up to 8192 data points and predicting up to 1024 at once. With the project we wanted to apply these models to, we were looking at thousands more data points at once. The next issue that I had noticed was the error rates of each model. By calculating the root mean square error of each prediction, I found that, even at its best, both models never got below ~1.5% difference, as seen in the eccentricity heatmap graph. Using these graphs to fuel my decision, I had chosen not to use these models for the main project. My research concluded with a presentation to the firm about the current state of TSFMs, my graphs, what they represented, and most importantly, what models felt promising given a few more years of research and work.

Quicksilver Labs Newsletter Filter

Summer 2026

This project is a newsletter site that my supervisor wanted for the purpose of caching and dispatching the most relevant news to the firm. He gave me a description of the goal of the tool. From there, I designed a specification document, designed the workflow that a user would follow to produce and send a newsletter, and then implemented the tool. BeyondMaps

April 2026 - Present

A browser-based 3D solar system explorer with planet search and information. I decided to make this after watching Project Hail Mary as a fun side project. AI was used as an assistant for this, but only to learn about ReactJS and the API I was using. All code and design decisions were made by me. While this was a side-project, it also served to introduce me to coding with React and other frameworks. My biggest goal for this project was to create a similar product to Google Maps, but for the solar system instead. This is what motivated my choice of interface to closely replicate the standard Google had set. Something I learned quickly after beginning this project was the difference between code and presentation. After writing the code, all of my math and layout would be correct. However, by viewing my tool, it was clear that many moons still sat within their parent planet, and the sheer scale of space would be hard to represent easily. In the future, I plan to work on planet and moon scale, moon placement relative to planet, and an alternate version to explore planets easier. This is to create a version where users can more easily navigate cross-planet without requiring the search bar.

How students use AI while studying

March - May 2026

In User-Centered Research & Evaluation at Carnegie Mellon, my team explored how AI could support step-by-step studying and independent reasoning rather than simply provide answers. I wrote the interview guide, recruited and conducted contextual interviews with two participants, and contributed to interpretation sessions, affinity diagramming, and the final report and recommendations. Our interviews focused on specific recent experiences with AI during coursework, including what led participants to seek help and how they felt about the assistance they received.

In our sample, participants described turning to AI when other academic resources fell short and time pressure or fatigue increased. Some wanted a hint or help finding an error, but received a full solution instead. These findings pointed to a tension between finishing an assignment and retaining control over the learning process. Our team recommended designing and testing a study tool with adjustable levels of assistance, such as hints, partial steps, and error checking. This project produced research findings and recommendations; we did not implement or test the proposed tool, and the findings should not be generalized to all students.

Design of AI Products

Spring 2026

In this class, we were given between a week and two weeks to come up with an idea using a given application of AI. The final presentation for each group was a pitch for our technology, which is included below. Each project also has a process book included, which shows the timeline and process my group and I completed our work in.

  • Health Connect
    Pitch · Process book · Demo video

    For this project, my team and I designed AI agent that is designed to be used by medical professionals aimed to reduce patient discharge time and errors. It would create a connection between patient and doctor to order prescriptions, book appointments, and dispatch special equipment to home addresses. For this project, I researched the technical and financial feasibility of our agent. With this information, it helped steer our project towards a more cost-effective product with a large addressable market while still recognizing a special pipeline that medical tools must follow to adhere to HIPAA protocols.

  • ContractClarity
    Pitch · Process book

    ContractClarity is a proposed tool for DocuSign that would clarify confusing legal terms, summarize long contracts, and identify potentially predatory or unusual language in documents. Throughout the project, I helped brainstorm ideas given two capabilities of AI and create a risk matrix for all ideas across the group. Once we had landed on ContractClarity, I helped identify and avoid potential ethical issues as well as determine the financial feasibility of this product. During the ideation phase, my brainstorming and concept cards helped give direction to our eventual product. My research showed the capabilities of different AI applications while highlighting the flaws it contained. With this information, my team and I were able to accurately assess the risk of my potential project direction to determine the best way to proceed with our project.

  • Canvas QuickClick
    Pitch · Process book

    For Canvas QuickClick, my team and I proposed a redesign of the mobile navigation flow of Instructure's Canvas platform that surfaced the most frequently visited course pages and materials, designed to reduce the number of navigation steps required in the most often followed user flows. Throughout this research, I helped my team brainstorm augmented user interfaces for various apps. After ranking each idea and selecting Canvas, my team and I drafted a value flow model between students, teachers, and schools using Canvas. This helped inform us of the gap we were aiming to fill, and where each stakeholder stood within this redesign. Finally, this project concluded with our low-fidelity and mid-fidelity mockups, where I helped organize our pitch and financial feasibility into a story that the viewer could follow along with more easily.

  • HandiTools
    Process book

    HandiTools was the first project of this class, and the only project that I completely owned. I started by creating, researching, and matchmaking potential ideas. After receiving critique from my classmates, I selected one idea and refined it into a single pitch slide. My product was accessible power tools, where a proposed change would be that touch-sensitive surfaces would detect when an area (i.e. a trigger) of a power tool has been gripped, allowing people with physical disabilities or reduced motor control to use power tools. I showcased the ideas capabilities, its co-creation of value, and overall feasibility in a final slide. This project introduced me to matchmaking during the ideation process, which guided how I approached each idea with a more broad view to avoid getting locked into a local maximum.
